## 1. What we set out to test

Sanjeev's modification of OPSD (Kaur/Ri/He/Fowl/Arora, arXiv 2607.05184). The
paper: a frozen self-teacher given privileged context (gold solution) degrades
thinking models at long rollout budgets. His idea:

* teacher context = problem + **two UNLABELED student responses**, one correct
  (x⁺) and one wrong (x⁻), order randomized. Template, verbatim:
  `"Here are two examples of student responses to the question:\n{a}\n \n{b}\n \n
  Now answer with a response of your own, including the thinking process."`
  Rationale: labelling one correct makes the teacher ignore the other.
* loss **gated to wrong rollouts only** (correct rollouts are where the paper's
  unproductive shortening comes from), batch kept full by regeneration.
* mental model: OPSD = poor man's RL; the student's rollout **masquerades** as
  the teacher's own in-progress reasoning.

## 2. Headline result: Arm 1 fails, confounds closed

avg@16 at the 38,912-token budget, Qwen3-4B:

| | AIME24 | AIME25 | HMMT25 | Avg | Δ base |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base (ours) | 0.756 | 0.648 | 0.394 | 0.599 | — |
| dense-gold control | 0.679 | 0.592 | 0.356 | 0.542 | **−5.7** |
| Arm 1 (5836 probs, 91 steps) | 0.581 | 0.473 | 0.300 | 0.451 | −14.8 |
| **Arm 1 matched** (3661 probs, 57 steps — same data+steps as control) | 0.621 | 0.483 | 0.292 | **0.465** | **−13.4** |

* Our control reproduces the paper EXACTLY (their base avg 0.591, their OPSD
  −5.7). Pipeline validated.
* Matching data and steps recovered only 1.4 of the 9-pt gap → **the method
  causes the extra damage**, not the confounds.
* **Rollout length collapses −32% within ~28 steps and plateaus** (control: −8%
  on identical data). Eval traces 22–24% shorter. Budget curves cross between 8k
  and 16k: Arm 1 is BETTER at 4–8k, worse at ≥16k. That is the paper's own
  pathology, amplified.
* The gate worked (`gate/live_fraction` 0.78–0.92). Not a plumbing failure.

## 3. Why: the OPSD teacher cannot think

**The key architectural insight** (surprised Sanjeev too). The teacher never
generates — training only takes forward passes `P_T(y_t | ctx, y_<t)` over the
STUDENT's tokens. So adjudicating the unlabeled pair must happen implicitly in a
single forward pass. The paper never faced this because gold needs no
adjudication.

Adjudication probe, 300 pairs × {none, unlabeled, neglabel} × {think, nothink},
scored on clean pairs only:

| | Countdown* | math |
|---|---|---|
| picks right when copying a candidate, no thinking | (void, see §5) | **54.4%** |
| …with thinking | (void) | **62.8%** |

* ~91% of the time the model just copies one of the two candidates. Context
  raises accuracy 0.182 → 0.511 by **answer-hinting**, not adjudication, and
  bundles that with early commitment (responses roughly halve).
* **Explicit negative labels BACKFIRE**: told an answer is wrong, the model picks
  it *more* (math: 45.8% vs 45.2%). This kills Arm 1c independently.
* Claude Opus 5 on the same pairs: **74%** (floor; ≥2 of its 13 "errors" were our
  labels being wrong). So even a frontier model is far from reliable here.

**Conclusion (Sanjeev's framing):** contrastive-OPSD needs a
**verification–generation gap** — the teacher must grade reliably even when it
cannot solve. For competition math the gap is narrow: checking a half-page
derivation ≈ redoing it. Viability is a property of the (model, task) pair and is
**measurable in advance by this probe, ~1 GPU-hour, no training**.

## 4. Two grader bugs found (both affect the PAPER, not just us)

**(a) math — semantic, 11.1%.** `math_equal` mislabels answers that are equal but
written differently: `x` vs `p(x)=x`, `\mathbb{Z}` vs `Z`, `\det(A)>0` vs `>0`,
`\mu_2` vs `\{1,-1\}`. Measured on all 45,482 unique (gold, predicted) pairs.
Fix shipped: `evaluation/llm_equal.py` — batched g-flash equivalence, exact/numeric
fast path, disk cache, coverage-verified with retry, math_equal fallback. 200
pairs/call, ~$0.02/1000. Wired into `build_pool.py` (`--no_llm_relabel` to skip).
Verdict cache for the whole corpus is on della at `harvest/llm_equal_cache.json`
(36,847 entries) so rebuilds need no network.

**(b) Countdown — syntactic, 14.5% of ALL rollouts.** `CountdownTask.grade` fed
the raw box to a guard `^[\d+\-*/().]+$` that forbids `=`. So
`\boxed{92+5-66=31}` scored 0 while `\boxed{92-66+5}` scored 1 — correctness
never evaluated. 16.5% of rollouts box the `expr = result` form → 14.5% false
negatives overall (64.4% among graded-wrong), understating accuracy 92.0% → 77.5%.
**Fixed** in `b0de1b4` (both branches now strip at `=`; 6 regression cases pass).

> **POSSIBLE CONFOUND IN THE PAPER'S COUNTDOWN ARMS — worth telling Simran.**
> OPSD training uses no reward, so this touched only the `countdown_38912` EVAL.
> But the dense context field (`response_suffix`) contains `=` in **100%** of
> rows while the sparse field (`response_answer_only`) contains it in **0%**. If
> students imitate their teacher's format, dense-trained students write `=` more
> and get penalised by the bug, sparse-trained students less — which would
> manufacture or amplify the reported dense-worse-than-sparse gradient. NOT
> demonstrated, but right sign and plausible magnitude. Cheap test, no retraining:
> take their Countdown checkpoints' stored eval generations, measure the `=` rate
> by arm, re-grade with the fixed grader, see if the gap narrows.

## 6. Agreed next step: train the model to be a better grader (RL)

Sanjeev's definition — NOT a verdict emitter:
> given (right answer, wrong answer, partial thinking trace) → produce
> (completed thinking trace, right answer)

Why this is the right object: output stays *think-then-answer*, so no verdict
register to leak into ordinary solving, no negation phrasing to invert, and it
trains **exactly** the quantity OPSD consumes (`P(next | ctx, prefix)`) rather
than hoping adjudication shows up in the conditionals.

Design points already agreed:
* **Harden the task, don't filter it.** Filtering by difficulty conditions on
  model failure → biased sub-distribution. Countdown with **5–6 numbers** keeps
  verification a one-line `eval()` while pushing generation back into difficulty.
  Generator does not exist yet; build solvable-by-construction instances, and fix
  the prompt to "each number exactly once" (current prompt says "at most once"
  but the grader demands all — 7.7% of x⁻ were subset solutions).
* **Prefixes of varied length, up to ~½ the trace**, so the model has "enough
  steam left to change direction". Aligns with OPSD's own loss window (first 4,096
  of ~10,900 tokens ≈ 38%).
* **Reward is outcome-only** (last `\boxed{}` only; the trace is never graded).
  Therefore the reward is BLIND to the main failure mode: **ignore the prefix and
  re-solve** earns full reward while teaching nothing about steering. Must be a
  separate *measurement* (does the completion repair the prefix's state or
  silently restart?), not a reward term.
* KL budget on the generative distribution, measured on the rollouts OPSD will
  later score; consider LoRA so teacher-with/without-adapter is an ablation.
* Two-sided eval from the start: verdict/completion accuracy AND AIME/HMMT
  avg@16 + response length + fork rate. **Length was the leading indicator of
  damage in Arm 1, visible long before accuracy.**

**Blockers before GPU time:** (1) re-harvest result, (2) hard-Countdown generator,
(3) written spec — Sanjeev asked for one, (4) no RL trainer in this repo; `verl`
lives in `della-post-training`, so it is a cross-repo setup.

## 7. Repo state / gotchas

* v3 math pools (LLM-relabelled + consistency filter): `pool_numina_v3.jsonl`
  (3,073), `pool_deepmath_v3.jsonl` (4,385) → `contrastive_arm1_pooled_v3.parquet`
  (4,823 rows ≈ 75 steps). **`add_gold_solutions.py` NOT yet rerun for v3** — the
  dense-gold control needs it.
* `arm1_launch.sh {arm1|baseline}` — `DATASET_NAME` env override is REQUIRED for
  reruns; `slurm/train.sh` skips training entirely if the checkpoint exists and
  would silently re-evaluate the old model.
* Eval shards must run with `SKIP_GENERATE_MERGE=true` then `./merge_evals.sh
  <ckpt>` — otherwise array worker 0 becomes a merge supervisor and holds a GPU at
  0% for up to 5.5h (job 12828716 sat 2h13m and drew a cluster warning).
* **della kills any process idle 90 min**, GPU or not. All CPU stages run inside
  GPU jobs under `pilot/gpu_keepalive.py` (continuous, not bursty — a 60s-sleep
  version would read 0% to the sampler).
* **Never grade inside a vLLM worker.** `math_equal` forks to implement its
  timeout; forking a CUDA-context holder deadlocked shard 0 of job 12413497 and
  cost 5h of an 8-GPU node. Generate with `--no_grade`, grade on CPU.
* `grade_jsonl.py` must pass the WHOLE row as `item` (CountdownTask reads
  `datapoint_x` from it; `{}` scores every Countdown rollout wrong), and its dedup
  key must include `cell` (probe files carry 6 rows per question/generation).
* OpenRouter key is on the LAPTOP (`~/.zshrc`), not della, and della needs a proxy
  module for outbound → judge locally, ship the cache. See
  [[reference_openrouter_models]]; current Gemini is `google/gemini-3.7-flash`.
* **Methodological lesson worth keeping:** my Countdown label audit reported 0.0%
  false negatives when the truth was 92% — because `independent_grade`
  reimplemented the *same* `=` blind spot as the grader it audited. An auditor
  written against the same assumption is not an audit. What caught it was printing
  raw boxed strings. Same shape as the daemonic-Pool grader bug and the eval merge
  that globbed whatever shards existed: **output-reading code that fails quietly
  and looks like a result.**
